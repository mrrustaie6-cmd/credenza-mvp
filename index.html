AI in this demo

How the prototype uses AI

This part is deliberately narrow and transparent: it classifies synthetic transaction patterns, highlights unusual behaviour for review, and organizes evidence into a single, explainable signal. Nothing here is a final credit verdict. The current implementation is a mock, rule-based prototype; no trained production model is used in this repository.

01 / Labeling

Transaction classification

With fully synthetic examples, the system can assign labels such as regular, unusual, or needs review based on simple transaction features, timing, and amount patterns.

02 / Review triggers

Anomaly flags

Unusual behaviour can be surfaced for human inspection, such as repeated small transfers, large cash-out patterns, or inconsistent source records that deserve context.

03 / Human control

Decision remains with people

Credenza does not decide whether someone is fit for a loan or bank account. It organizes evidence and flags what needs human attention.

Process

From invisible to reviewable

The workflow below is a proposed design, not a live production system. It shows how evidence could be organized for review and decision support, without claiming that the current repo has implemented a real operating bank workflow.

01 / Consent

Person opts in

The newcomer chooses exactly which sources to connect. Nothing is pulled without an explicit, revocable grant.

02 / Verify

Signals cross-checked

Verified financial records, guarantors and registries are checked against each other, not taken at face value — on-chain history is one additional signal among several, not the core truth source.

03 / Score

Experimental signal assembled

A single, explainable score is produced for demonstration purposes only — every point is traceable to a signal and data quality flag.

04 / Review

Human decides

The destination bank or reviewer decides whether the signal is relevant and adequate. The AI is a support tool, not the final adjudicator.

Built for verification, not blind trust

Credenza does not ask a bank to trust a score blindly. Each signal behind the index is linked to its source, its verification status, and its confidence level. In production, the final decision remains with a human reviewer and the institution responsible for compliance.

System design

Architecture, in four layers

This is a proposed operating model. None of the steps below are currently deployed or active in the public demo. The purpose is to show a plausible review-and-evidence flow, not a live implementation.

Layer 1

Consent Layer

Per-source, revocable authorization. Every data pull is logged against an explicit user grant — nothing is fetched by default.

→
Layer 2

Corridor Verification Engine

Source-specific adapters extract and cross-check signals (documents, guarantors, registries, on-chain history) against each other.

→
Layer 3

Trust Scoring Engine

Converts verified signals into a single explainable index, with each contribution traceable back to its source and confidence level.

→
Layer 4

Bank API Layer

Delivers the evidence-backed profile to the destination bank's onboarding or underwriting flow via a versioned REST API.

Data layer is non-custodial by design: Credenza is built to broker verification and consent, not to become a long-term store of applicants' raw financial documents.

Compliance & data posture

Designed around consent, not around access

Principles the architecture is being designed to, ahead of any real integration. These are not guarantees or legal claims — they define the intended safety model.

✓

Consent-first by default

No source is queried without an explicit, per-source, revocable authorization from the person.

§

GDPR-aligned handling

Data minimization, purpose limitation and a documented legal basis are treated as design requirements, not afterthoughts.

⌘

Non-custodial data layer

Credenza is built to broker and verify, not to become a permanent warehouse of applicants' raw financial documents.

⇄

Evidence, not a black box

Banks receive the signal-level evidence behind the index, and always make the final underwriting decision with human oversight.

Concept note

Why this, why now

Global credit-portability products already exist and already work — banks pay for them. Existing providers have limited reach in certain corridor situations and often do not fit the reality of migrants whose financial history is real but fragmented across borders.

This page is a concept demonstration prepared alongside the written business plan for evaluation by Startup Estonia's Startup Committee. It is intentionally explicit that the project is pre-operational and not a live credit product.

Current status — please read

Credenza is pre-operational. It does not currently have live operational access to real financial data in Iran, or in any other market. Everything on this page — the interactive score, the sample profile builder, the source weights, and the mock API output — is illustrative and intended to show how the concept could work in a controlled pilot environment.
